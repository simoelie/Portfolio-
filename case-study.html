01 / POWER BI

Business Intelligence Dashboard

Business problem & objective

Commercial results and opportunity stages are difficult to interpret when viewed in separate monthly and regional summaries. The report brings these views together so a user can inspect patterns and identify questions for follow-up.

My role, inputs & tools

Portfolio BI report. Inputs shown in the public dashboard include opportunities, revenue, months, regions and pipeline stages. Power BI is confirmed by the published report. The underlying source, schema, Power Query steps and DAX expressions are not public, so I do not claim specific transformations or measures.

Approach & methodology

Define a decision question, align KPI definitions, check grain and missing values, explore trends and segment comparisons, then test whether visual choices make exceptions clear. A documented implementation would include a star model, reusable measures and data quality checks where appropriate.

Insights, recommendations & impact

Users can compare monthly and regional performance and inspect pipeline distribution. A practical next step is to investigate unusually low conversion or uneven regional patterns against source records before acting. The public report alone does not establish a quantified commercial outcome.

Lessons learned

A dashboard should expose definitions, data freshness and drill-down context alongside attractive visuals. Avoid treating a correlation in a chart as an explanation.

02 / BUSINESS ANALYSIS

Employee Absenteeism Process Optimization

This is an anonymized, illustrative requirements and process design based on a real operational problem. The sample artifacts below are proposed deliverables, not employer-approved documentation.

Business problem & objective

Unexpected absences can leave scheduling teams with too little time to arrange coverage. The objective is to shorten the time between notification, visibility and action while keeping responsibilities clear.

My role & stakeholders

Illustrative BA role: facilitate discovery, map the process, write testable requirements and align acceptance with managers. Stakeholders to consult: employees, scheduling coordinators, unit managers, HR and privacy/data teams.

AS-IS process & pain points

Absence reported→Manual relay→Manager reviews→Coverage arranged

Potential pain points to validate: fragmented channels, late visibility, ambiguous ownership and inconsistent handoffs. No claim is made that every step occurred exactly this way at the employer.

Root cause & TO-BE process

Use interviews, a swimlane map and a cause-and-effect review to distinguish notification delays from staffing constraints.

Structured report→Notify owner→Confirm receipt→Escalate & track

Business & functional requirements

  • Capture absence notification with role-based access and a clear timestamp.
  • Notify the designated scheduling owner when a report arrives.
  • Show acknowledgement and escalation status without exposing unnecessary personal information.
  • Define retention and access with privacy stakeholders before implementation.

User story & acceptance criteria

As a scheduling coordinator, I want to see newly reported absences in one view so I can prioritize coverage.

  • Given a valid report, when it is submitted, then the designated owner sees a timestamped item.
  • Given no acknowledgement within the agreed window, when the threshold is reached, then an escalation is recorded.

KPIs & UAT strategy

Proposed KPIs: notification-to-acknowledgement time, coverage lead time, unfilled shifts and process exceptions. UAT should cover timely and late notices, duplicates, accessibility, permissions and failed notifications with representative users.

Recommendations, impact & lessons

Prototype the workflow with coordinators, agree on definitions and validate privacy controls. A better shared status and earlier prompts may improve response time; impact must be measured after implementation. Agile iteration and Lean Six Sigma tools support the review; BPMN can formalize the approved flow.

03 / WORKFORCE ANALYTICS

Workforce Analytics & Operational Performance

Challenge & business question

A notable rise in spontaneous absenteeism appeared over a short period in a healthcare operational context. Which trend, segment or process signal warrants attention, and what action is feasible?

My role, data & approach

The anonymized case combines operational needs, KPI definitions, trend review and stakeholder discussion. Internal records, identifiable employee details and exact unpublished counts are excluded here.

Methodology & tools

Check definitions and baseline periods; review rates and counts over time, team or shift where permitted; compare observed patterns with operational context; then validate possible explanations with managers. Power BI, Power Query and DAX supported BI work in this role.

Solution & key insight

A concise operational view helps separate a short-term spike from persistent patterns. The case emphasized earlier visibility into spontaneous absence and the practical timing of follow-up. This is an analytical observation, not a causal proof.

Recommendations & outcome

One recommendation was a reminder ahead of a shift. The situation subsequently returned toward normal in the original account, but a controlled causal effect and precise improvement figure are not established. Decision support and follow-up measurement are the defensible impact.

Lessons learned

Metric definitions, data privacy and operational feasibility matter as much as the chart. A manager needs an action window, not merely an after-the-fact total.

04 / ECONOMETRICS

Internal Migration & Native Labour Outcomes in South Africa

Thesis: Migration interne et marché du travail des non-migrants : une approche en panel instrumentée pour l’Afrique du Sud.

Research question & dataset

What is the causal effect of internal, especially rural-to-urban, migration on the wages and hours worked of non-migrant workers in South Africa? The thesis uses longitudinal National Income Dynamics Study (NIDS) data covering 2008–2010/2011.

My role, variables & preparation

I designed and conducted the economic research. Outcomes are native workers’ hourly wages and hours worked; the explanatory variable is the migration rate in destination districts. The analysis uses a long-difference panel approach and relevant controls.

Methodology

Spatial selection and reverse causality can bias ordinary comparisons. The instrumental-variable strategy uses a climate-based shift-share instrument combining standardized temperature anomalies in origin districts with historical migration shares. Identification depends on the instrument’s relevance and exclusion restriction.

Results & interpretation

The instrumental-variable estimates find no statistically significant effect of rural-to-urban migration on native hourly wages. They suggest a modest positive response in native hours worked to a one-percentage-point increase in the rural-to-urban migration rate. Consult the thesis for specifications, estimates and uncertainty.

Policy implications & lessons

The findings point to adjustment through hours rather than wages in the study setting. The assumptions behind a climate-based instrument, district-level variation and the time period must be considered before broader policy conclusions.
